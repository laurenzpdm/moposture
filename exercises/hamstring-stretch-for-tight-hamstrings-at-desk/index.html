Standing hamstring stretch for tight hamstrings: how to do it

Sitting for hours keeps the muscles behind your thighs short, and many people feel it as tightness when they stand up or bend forward. The standing hamstring stretch needs only a low step or bench and takes about 40 seconds.

It will not fix back pain on its own. Guidance on back pain generally recommends staying active and moving regularly, and short movement breaks are an easy way to do that.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Stand tall and rest one heel on a low step or bench, leg straight, toes up.
  2. Hinge forward from your hips with a long back until you feel a stretch behind your thigh.
  3. Hold, then switch legs.

Dose: 20 seconds per side.

Tip: Keep the pressure feather-light and your shoulders down.

Common mistakes

  • Rounding your lower back to reach further. Hinge at the hips and keep your back long.
  • Locking or hyperextending the knee. Keep the leg straight but relaxed.
  • Bouncing. Hold the position still.
  • Holding your breath or pushing into pain. A mild stretch is enough.

Easier options

  • Use a lower surface, or keep a slight bend in the knee.
  • Hold for 10 seconds instead of 20.
  • Rest your hands on your thigh for balance and hinge only a little.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in a leg or foot
  • pain that shoots down a leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • problems controlling your bladder or bowels (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss

If the stretch causes sharp or pulling pain, stop and try the lying knee rock guide instead, or ask a professional.
