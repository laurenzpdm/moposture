Tight hips from sitting: 3 gentle moves

Long hours in a chair keep the hips bent, and many people notice tight hips or a stiff lower back when they finally stand up. These three slow moves take about three minutes. Start with the seated figure-four at your desk, then do the other two on the floor when you can. Keep everything comfortable.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

Seated figure-four

  1. Sit tall on a chair with both feet flat on the floor.
  2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee.
  3. Keep your back long and hinge forward from the hips until you feel a stretch, then switch.

Dose: 25 second hold per side.

Tip: Press lightly on the raised knee only if it feels comfortable.

Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

  1. Kneel on your right knee with a cushion beneath it and your left foot forward.
  2. Tuck your tailbone slightly and shift forward until you feel a stretch at the front of your right hip.
  3. Hold with your torso tall, then switch sides.

Dose: 20 second hold per side.

Tip: Squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg to deepen the stretch without arching.

Glute bridge

  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat, hip-width apart.
  2. Press through your heels and lift your hips until shoulders, hips and knees form a line.
  3. Squeeze your glutes for 2 seconds, then lower slowly.

Dose: about 10 reps.

Tip: Stop the lift where your ribs stay down. Do not arch your back.

Common mistakes

  • Arching the lower back in the hip flexor stretch. Tuck the tailbone a little instead.
  • Rounding your back to get deeper in the figure-four. Hinge from the hips with a long back.
  • Rushing the bridge. Lift and lower slowly.
  • Pushing into pain. Reduce the range or stop.

Do the three moves once a day, or after a long stretch of sitting. Standing up and walking for a minute or two every so often helps too.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist, or seek urgent care where noted, if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in a leg
  • pain that spreads down a leg below the knee
  • pain that started after a fall or other injury, or you cannot put weight on the leg
  • numbness around your genitals or bottom, or new trouble controlling your bladder or bowels (seek urgent care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
