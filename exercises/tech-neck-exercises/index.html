Tech neck exercises: 3 gentle moves

Looking down at a phone or laptop for long stretches can leave the neck and upper back feeling tired. These three short moves are a simple place to start. Each takes under a minute.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

Chin tuck

  1. Sit or stand tall and look straight ahead.
  2. Glide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then relax.

Dose: 8 repetitions, hold each for 3 seconds.

Tip: Move slowly. You should feel a light stretch at the base of your skull, never pain.

Neck side stretch

  1. Sit tall and let both shoulders drop away from your ears.
  2. Tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder until you feel a gentle stretch.
  3. Breathe slowly, then switch sides.

Dose: Hold for 20 seconds per side.

Tip: Do not pull on your head. Let its weight do the work.

Shoulder blade squeeze

  1. Sit or stand tall with your arms relaxed and palms facing forward.
  2. Draw your shoulder blades back and down, as if tucking them into your back pockets.
  3. Hold for 5 seconds, then release. Repeat 8 times without shrugging.

Dose: 8 repetitions, hold each for 5 seconds.

Tip: Think of a gentle squeeze, not a hard pinch.

Common mistakes

  • Doing too much at once. A few calm reps beat a long session.
  • Forcing a stretch. Everything here should feel mild.
  • Treating exercises as the only fix. Raising your screen toward eye level and taking short breaks also helps.
  • Ignoring warning signs. Pain that spreads or comes with numbness needs a professional.

Do the three moves in order, once or twice a day, or after long phone sessions.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
