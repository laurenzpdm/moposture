Stiff neck: 3 gentle moves to ease tension

A neck that feels tight after long hours at a screen or an awkward night can make turning your head uncomfortable. These three short moves stretch the sides and back of the neck and train a calmer head position. Start with the side stretch, then add the other two.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

Neck side stretch

  1. Sit tall and let both shoulders drop away from your ears.
  2. Tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder until you feel a gentle stretch.
  3. Breathe slowly, then switch sides.

Dose: 20 second hold per side.

Tip: Do not pull on your head. Let its weight do the work.

Neck diagonal stretch

  1. Sit tall and turn your head about 45 degrees to the right.
  2. Look down toward your right pocket and rest your right hand lightly on the back of your head.
  3. Let the weight of your hand add a gentle stretch, then switch sides.

Dose: 20 second hold per side.

Tip: Keep the pressure feather-light and your shoulders down.

Chin tuck

  1. Sit or stand tall and look straight ahead.
  2. Glide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then relax.

Dose: 8 repetitions.

Tip: Move slowly. You should feel a light stretch at the base of your skull, never pain.

Common mistakes

  • Pulling on your head. Let gravity and a feather-light hand do the work.
  • Raising the shoulder on the side you stretch. Keep both shoulders away from your ears.
  • Holding your breath. Slow breathing helps the neck muscles let go.
  • Stretching into sharp pain. A mild stretch is enough, so ease off if it pinches.

Do the three moves in order, once or twice a day, or whenever your neck feels tight. Short breaks to stand up and look away from the screen also help.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist, or call emergency services where noted, if you notice:

  • a stiff neck together with a high temperature, severe headache, vomiting, a rash, or discomfort when looking at bright lights (urgent medical help)
  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or hand
  • neck pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • pain that keeps getting worse, spreads down your arm, or does not improve after a few weeks
