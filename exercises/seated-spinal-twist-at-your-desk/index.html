Seated spinal twist at your desk: how to do it

The seated spinal twist is a simple mobility move: you sit tall and rotate gently from side to side. It takes about 45 seconds and needs only a chair, so it fits between emails or video calls.

It will not fix back pain on its own. Guidance on neck and back pain generally recommends staying active and moving regularly, and short movement breaks are an easy way to do that.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Sit tall near the front of a chair with your feet flat.
  2. Cross your arms over your chest and rotate gently to the right, keeping your hips still.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, return, then rotate to the left.

Dose: about 6 slow reps, 3 to each side.

Tip: Turn from your ribcage, not your neck. Keep both sit bones on the seat.

Common mistakes

  • Twisting from the neck. Turn your whole ribcage and keep your chin level.
  • Letting the hips swing. Keep both sit bones on the seat.
  • Holding your breath. Breathe out as you turn.
  • Pushing into pain. Turn less far or stop.

Easier options

  • Turn only part of the way.
  • Skip the 3-second hold.
  • Rest your hands on your thighs instead of crossing your arms.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or hand
  • pain that spreads down an arm
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • chest pain or shortness of breath (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss

If twisting causes sharp pain in your back, stop and try the knee-to-chest stretch or open book guide instead, or ask a professional.
