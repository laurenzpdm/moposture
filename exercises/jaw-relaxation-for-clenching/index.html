Jaw relaxation for clenching

If you catch yourself clenching, the jaw resting position is a simple reset: lips together, teeth apart, tongue resting up. It is a habit you can check in on throughout the day.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Let your lips rest together and your back teeth come apart.
  2. Rest the tip of your tongue on the roof of your mouth, just behind your front teeth.
  3. Breathe slowly through your nose and let your jaw feel heavy.

Dose: Hold for 20 seconds.

Tip: Your teeth should only touch when you chew or swallow. Check in with this position during the day.

Common mistakes

  • Pressing the tongue hard against the roof of the mouth. It should rest lightly.
  • Keeping the teeth lightly touching. Teeth should only meet when you chew or swallow.
  • Forcing the jaw open or sideways. Keep it relaxed and heavy.
  • Ignoring ongoing jaw pain or clicking that is getting worse. Ask a dentist or doctor.

Put a small reminder note on your screen. Check your jaw whenever you read it.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
  • jaw that locks open or closed, or you cannot open it fully
  • swelling in the face or jaw, or pain with fever
