Woke up with a stiff neck? What helps, step by step

You turn your head in the morning and it catches. The NHS lists a neck "locked in an awkward position while sleeping" as one of the most common causes of neck pain, and it usually settles on its own. Here is what the NHS advises for the first days, three gentle moves to keep the neck moving, and the signs that mean you should get it checked.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

The first days: what the NHS advises

  • Keep it moving. Gentle movement and neck flexibility exercises, rather than a collar, unless a doctor tells you otherwise.
  • Heat or cold. A hot water bottle wrapped in a tea towel for 20 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day, or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a tea towel for 5 minutes, 3 times a day.
  • Pain relief. Paracetamol or ibuprofen, or ibuprofen gel rubbed on the neck. Check the leaflet and ask a pharmacist if you are unsure.
  • Tonight's pillow. A low, firm pillow, head level with the rest of your body, a firm mattress, and try not to sleep on your front.
  • Careful with driving. Skip driving or cycling if you cannot turn your head safely to check behind you.

Most neck pain only lasts a few weeks, according to the NHS.

Three gentle moves for this morning

Do them in this order, slowly, and only within a range that feels comfortable. Repeat once or twice more during the day.

Shoulder rolls

  1. Stand or sit tall with your arms relaxed.
  2. Lift your shoulders toward your ears, then roll them back and down in a big circle.
  3. After half the reps, reverse the direction.

Dose: 10 slow circles.

Tip: Start here. It warms up the area without asking the neck to turn.

Neck side stretch

  1. Sit tall and let both shoulders drop away from your ears.
  2. Tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder only as far as feels comfortable.
  3. Breathe slowly, then switch sides.

Dose: 20 second hold per side.

Tip: If one side is much stiffer, go less far on that side. Do not pull on your head.

Chin tuck

  1. Sit or stand tall and look straight ahead.
  2. Glide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then relax.

Dose: 8 repetitions.

Tip: Small and slow is fine. You should feel a light stretch at the base of your skull, never pain.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing the turn to "crack" it free. Work in the range you have and let it grow over the day.
  • Keeping the neck completely still. Stiffness tends to linger when you avoid all movement.
  • Hunching over the phone to look things up. Hold the screen closer to eye level for a day or two.
  • Going back to the same pillow setup. If your pillow is very high or very soft, try a lower, firmer one tonight.

When to stop and see a professional

See a GP or physiotherapist, or get urgent help where noted, if:

  • the pain or stiffness does not go away after a few weeks, or painkillers have not worked
  • you have pins and needles, numbness or weakness in an arm or hand, or the arm feels cold
  • the stiffness came with a high temperature, severe headache, vomiting, a rash or discomfort when looking at bright lights (get urgent medical help)
  • the pain started after a fall, accident or other injury
