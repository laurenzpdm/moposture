Chin tucks for forward head posture

A chin tuck is a small, slow movement that glides your head back over your shoulders. It is one of the most common gentle exercises for a head that drifts forward during desk and phone time.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Sit or stand tall and look straight ahead.
  2. Glide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then relax.

Dose: 8 repetitions, hold each for 3 seconds.

Tip: Move slowly. You should feel a light stretch at the base of your skull, never pain.

Common mistakes

  • Tilting the head down or up instead of gliding it straight back. Keep your eyes level.
  • Pushing hard. A chin tuck should feel like a light stretch at the base of the skull, not strain.
  • Clenching the jaw or holding your breath. Keep your jaw soft and keep breathing.
  • Rushing through reps. Slow and smooth is the point.

Try a set before you start work and another after lunch. Two fingers on the chin can help you feel the straight line.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
