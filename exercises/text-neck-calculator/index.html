General information, not medical advice. This is a modeled estimate, not a measurement of your neck, and it cannot tell you whether you have or will develop a problem.

Where the numbers come from

A 2014 paper by spine surgeon Kenneth Hansraj estimated the forces on the cervical spine as the head tilts forward. An adult head weighs about 10 to 12 pounds, and the reported forces rise quickly with the angle:

Head tiltModeled load
0° (neutral)about 10 to 12 lb (5 kg)
15°27 lb (12 kg)
30°40 lb (18 kg)
45°49 lb (22 kg)
60°60 lb (27 kg)

Those five rows are the reported values. For 5°, 10° and the other in-between angles, the calculator draws a straight line between the two nearest rows, so those results are our estimate, not published figures.

What this does not tell you

  • It is a model. The figures come from a calculation, not from measurements inside real necks, and they cannot show how one person differs from another.
  • A high number is not a diagnosis. It says nothing about the health of your own neck.
  • The link to pain is unsettled. One study of 150 people aged 18 to 21 found no association between neck posture while texting and neck pain. It was a single cross-sectional study, so it does not settle the question for longer use or other age groups.

The useful takeaway is modest: if your neck feels tired after long phone sessions, changing position more often is an easy thing to try.

What you can try

  • Bring the phone up toward eye level. Lower angles give lower numbers in the table above.
  • Take short breaks and change position, rather than hunting for one perfect posture.
  • Try a few gentle moves. Our tech neck exercises take under a minute each, and chin tucks are a simple place to start.

When to see a professional

Speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
