Cat-cow stretch for lower back stiffness

Cat-cow is a gentle, breath-led movement that takes your spine through small arches and rounds. Many people use it as a warm-up when their back feels stiff after sitting.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders and knees under hips.
  2. Breathe in: let your belly sink and lift your chest and tailbone slightly.
  3. Breathe out: round your spine toward the ceiling and tuck your chin.

Dose: 8 slow breath cycles.

Tip: Move with your breath and keep the range small if your wrists feel sore.

Common mistakes

  • Going for a big range. Small and smooth is enough, especially at first.
  • Moving faster than your breath. Let the breath set the pace.
  • Letting your wrists take too much strain. Keep the range small if they feel sore, or try it seated on a chair.
  • Pushing into pain. Stay in a comfortable range.

No floor? Do it seated: hands on your knees, arch gently as you breathe in, round as you breathe out.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
