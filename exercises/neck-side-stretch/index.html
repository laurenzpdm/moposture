Gentle neck side stretch

The neck side stretch uses the weight of your own head to gently lengthen the side of your neck and top of the shoulder. It is an easy one to do in a chair, and the gentle version below needs no hand on your head at all. Some people call it a lateral neck stretch or an ear-to-shoulder stretch.

General information, not medical advice. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.

How to do it

  1. Sit tall and let both shoulders drop away from your ears.
  2. Tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder until you feel a gentle stretch.
  3. Breathe slowly, then switch sides.

Dose: Hold for 20 seconds per side.

Tip: Do not pull on your head. Let its weight do the work.

What you should feel

A mild, even pull along the side of your neck and the top of the shoulder on the side away from the tilt. It should feel easy enough that you can breathe slowly the whole time. You do not need to bring your ear close to your shoulder; a small tilt is plenty.

It should not feel sharp, pinching or tingling, and nothing should run down your arm. If it does, make the tilt smaller or stop.

Easier and next-step options

  • Easier: shrug and release. Lift both shoulders toward your ears, hold for 2 seconds, then let them drop. 8 reps. A good warm-up if tilting your head feels like too much today.
  • A slightly different angle: the upper trap stretch. Hold the edge of your seat with one hand, tilt the other way and turn your nose slightly down. 20 seconds per side.
  • Next step: the neck diagonal stretch. Turn your head about 45 degrees, look toward your pocket and let a feather-light hand add a little stretch. 20 seconds per side.

All three are part of the routines in the Mo app, with the same doses shown here.

Common mistakes

  • Lifting the shoulder up toward the ear. Let both shoulders drop.
  • Pulling on the head with your hand. Let gravity do the work.
  • Rotating or tilting the head forward. Keep the movement straight to the side.
  • Stretching into sharp pain, pinching or tingling. Ease off.

Breathe out as you tilt. Two slow breaths often feel better than a long, tense hold.

When to stop and see a professional

Stop and speak to a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice:

  • numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm, hand, leg or foot
  • pain that spreads (radiates) down an arm or leg
  • pain that started after an accident, fall or other injury
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness between your legs (seek emergency care)
  • pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
