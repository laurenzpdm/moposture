TMJ exercises at home: 6 gentle jaw moves

The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) connects your lower jaw to your skull. When it or the muscles around it cause trouble, the condition is often called TMD. The NHS says it is not usually serious and generally gets better on its own.

This short routine takes about 4.5 minutes and is meant to help you relax a tense jaw. It will not fix the cause of jaw pain. It is the same order Mo uses in the app's Jaw & TMJ relief session.

General information, not medical advice. Stay in a pain-free range and stop if pain increases. If you already have a diagnosis or a treatment plan, ask your dentist, doctor or physiotherapist which of these moves suit you.

The routine

1. Jaw resting position

  1. Let your lips rest together and your back teeth come apart.
  2. Rest the tip of your tongue on the roof of your mouth, just behind your front teeth.
  3. Breathe slowly through your nose and let your jaw feel heavy.

Dose: Hold for 20 seconds.

Tip: Your teeth should only touch when you chew or swallow. Check in with this position during the day.

2. Tongue-up jaw opening

  1. Rest your tongue tip on the roof of your mouth, behind your front teeth.
  2. Slowly open your jaw while the tongue stays up, only as far as feels easy.
  3. Close slowly along the same straight line.

Dose: 6 slow reps.

Tip: A mirror helps you keep the jaw straight. Never force the opening, and stop at any pain.

3. Chin tuck

  1. Sit or stand tall and look straight ahead.
  2. Glide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  3. Hold for 3 seconds, then relax.

Dose: 8 slow reps.

Tip: Move slowly. You should feel a light stretch at the base of your skull, never pain.

4. Side jaw glide

  1. Open your jaw just a little, about a fingertip's width.
  2. Slowly glide your lower jaw to the right, back to the middle, then to the left.
  3. Keep the movement small, smooth and pain-free.

Dose: 6 slow reps.

Tip: Tiny movements are enough. Skip any side that hurts or catches.

5. Jaw muscle massage

  1. Open your mouth slightly and find the muscle at the corner of your jaw with two or three fingertips.
  2. Make slow, small circles with light pressure, on both sides.
  3. Move gradually up toward your cheekbone and breathe out slowly.

Dose: 8 slow circles.

Tip: Light to medium pressure only. It should feel like relief, never sharp.

6. Belly breathing

  1. Rest a hand on your belly, lips together and teeth slightly apart.
  2. Breathe in slowly through your nose for about 4 seconds and let your belly rise.
  3. Breathe out gently for about 6 seconds and let your jaw and shoulders soften.

Dose: 6 slow breaths.

Tip: Keep your shoulders still and let each breath out be slow.

Everyday habits that can ease TMD

The NHS lists these self-help steps:

  • Eat soft food, like pasta, omelettes and soup.
  • Hold an ice pack or heat pack wrapped in a tea towel to the jaw, whichever feels better.
  • Massage the painful jaw muscles.
  • Find ways to relax.
  • Do not chew gum or pen tops, bite your nails or bite food with your front teeth.
  • Do not yawn too wide.
  • Do not clench your teeth. Apart from when eating, your teeth should be apart.

The NHS also mentions paracetamol or ibuprofen. Check the leaflet or ask a pharmacist whether they are right for you.

When to get checked

The NHS advises asking for an urgent GP appointment, or using NHS 111 in the UK, if you:

  • cannot eat or drink
  • have clicking, popping or grinding noises when you move your jaw
  • have difficulty opening your mouth fully, or your jaw is locking
  • have pain around your jaw, ear or at the side of your head
  • have frequent, severe headaches
  • have pain or tenderness at the side of your head or on the scalp
  • have vision problems, such as double vision or loss of vision in one or both eyes

Outside the UK, contact your doctor or dentist. A physiotherapist can advise on jaw exercises and massage, and a dentist can check whether teeth grinding might be involved.
